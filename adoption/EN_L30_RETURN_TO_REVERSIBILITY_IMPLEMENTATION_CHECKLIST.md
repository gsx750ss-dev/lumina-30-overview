# L30 Return-to-Reversibility Implementation Checklist

## How to use this checklist

Use this page after a boundary concern, incident review, pause, or stop condition has been identified. Each checked item should be backed by evidence, not merely assertion.

**Recommended review record:** for each section, record `Evidence`, `Gap`, `Action`, `Owner`, and `Re-entry status`.

## Practical Use Sequence

Use the [Practical Use Sequence](./EN_L30_RETURN_TO_REVERSIBILITY_PRACTICAL_USE_SEQUENCE.md) when the review needs a simple order: identify the boundary concern, preserve evidence, use this checklist, assign roles with the matrix, decide re-entry state, and keep long-term monitoring active.

## Role and Evidence Matrix

Use the [Return-to-Reversibility Role and Evidence Matrix](./EN_L30_RETURN_TO_REVERSIBILITY_ROLE_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md) when a review must identify who can maintain the stop, who preserves evidence, who restores effective refusal, who reviews re-entry, and what evidence makes those roles real rather than nominal.

## 1. Stop trigger and scope

- The stop or pause trigger is recorded, including why continued operation could weaken effective refusal, evidence, correction, or reversibility.
- The affected system, deployment, workflow, or decision pathway is clearly scoped.
- No additional irreversible escalation is allowed while review is pending.
- The authority to maintain the stop is identified and cannot be bypassed by the same actors seeking rapid re-entry.

## 2. Evidence preservation

- Decision records, warnings, approvals, refusal attempts, overrides, logs, and intervention windows are preserved.
- Missing evidence is recorded as a gap, not treated as proof that effective refusal existed.
- Evidence custody is assigned to a role that is not solely dependent on rapid re-entry incentives.
- The preserved record can be reviewed by a person or body capable of questioning re-entry.

## 3. Restore effective refusal

- The holder of human refusal authority is identified.
- Refusal can practically delay, reject, override, redirect, or stop the relevant trajectory before irreversible impact.
- Human judgment has enough time, information, and authority to affect the outcome.
- The process can distinguish actual refusal from merely apparent refusal.

## 4. Boundary diagnosis and corrective controls

- The boundary failure or near-failure is stated in operational terms.
- Corrective controls address the mechanism that weakened refusal, evidence, correction, rollback, or monitoring.
- The process is not returning to the same escalation path that produced the concern.
- The correction is testable, reviewable, and documented before re-entry is considered.

## 5. Controlled re-entry only

- Re-entry is limited in scope, time, dependency, and impact pathway.
- Rollback or renewed stop conditions are defined before re-entry begins.
- Monitoring is assigned for the re-entry window and cannot be treated as symbolic.
- Controlled re-entry is not described as certification, approval, legal compliance, or full restart of the previous trajectory.

## 6. Adversarial hollowing resistance

- Re-entry judgment does not rely solely on actors who benefit from speed, evidence minimization, or irreversible escalation.
- Evidence absence is treated as an unresolved review condition, not as clearance.
- The review is checked against ritualization: it must still be capable of stopping, refusing, or redirecting the trajectory.
- Apparent refusal, apparent reversibility, or apparent review is not treated as effective refusal, reversibility, or procedural validity.

## 7. Long-term continuity

- Roles for stop authority, evidence custody, boundary review, and re-entry review are defined for succession.
- Evidence retention and periodic boundary review are scheduled beyond the immediate incident.
- Stop triggers and renewed stop conditions are updated when systems, institutions, or technologies change.
- The ability to stop, verify, refuse, correct, and return remains alive across organizational change.

## Review outcome

| Outcome | Use this when |
| --- | --- |
| Do not re-enter | Effective refusal, evidence, corrective controls, or renewed stop conditions remain missing or unverifiable. |
| Limited controlled re-entry only | Minimum conditions are reviewable, but monitoring, scope limits, and rollback conditions remain necessary. |
| Continue monitoring | Re-entry has begun, but long-term reversibility, evidence retention, and anti-hollowing checks must remain active. |

**Boundary rule:** if actual refusal cannot be distinguished from apparent refusal, the process has not returned to reversibility.
