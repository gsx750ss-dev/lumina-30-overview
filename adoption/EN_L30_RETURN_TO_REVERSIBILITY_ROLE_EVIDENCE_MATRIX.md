# L30 Return-to-Reversibility Role and Evidence Matrix

## Purpose

The implementation checklist asks whether stop triggers, evidence, restored refusal, corrective controls, controlled re-entry, adversarial hollowing resistance, and long-term continuity are present. This matrix asks a narrower operational question: **who is responsible for each condition, and what evidence shows that the responsibility is real rather than merely nominal?**

**Boundary rule:** if no accountable role can preserve evidence, maintain the stop, challenge re-entry, or restore effective refusal, the process has not returned to reversibility.

## Role matrix

| Role | Minimum responsibility | Evidence to retain | Failure mode this prevents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stop Authority | Maintains the pause or stop condition until minimum return conditions are reviewable. | Stop decision, scope, timestamp, renewal conditions, who can lift or maintain the stop. | Review begins while irreversible escalation continues. |
| Evidence Custodian | Preserves logs, warnings, approvals, refusal attempts, overrides, intervention windows, and review records. | Evidence inventory, retention location, access controls, missing-evidence record. | Evidence disappears before refusal effectiveness can be verified. |
| Boundary Reviewer | Diagnoses what weakened refusal, correction, rollback, evidence, or reversibility. | Boundary diagnosis, same-path risk analysis, corrective-control record. | The process returns to the same irreversible escalation path. |
| Refusal Authority Holder | Has practical ability to delay, reject, override, redirect, or stop before irreversible impact. | Refusal mechanism, time window, authority scope, test or demonstration of actual effect. | Refusal exists only as formal approval or symbolic oversight. |
| Re-entry Reviewer | Reviews whether controlled re-entry is limited, monitored, reversible, and procedurally defensible. | Re-entry scope, monitoring plan, rollback condition, unresolved gaps, decision record. | Limited re-entry becomes a disguised full restart. |
| Monitoring Owner | Tracks renewed stop triggers and post-return reversibility after re-entry begins. | Monitoring window, escalation thresholds, renewed stop reports, post-return review record. | Re-entry resumes and then silently normalizes irreversible escalation. |
| Conflict-of-Interest Reviewer | Checks whether re-entry judgment depends only on actors who benefit from rapid return or evidence minimization. | Conflict review, independent challenge record, dissent or unresolved concern log. | Interested actors hollow out the return process. |

## Evidence matrix

| Review condition | Minimum evidence | Unresolved if missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stop trigger and scope | Recorded trigger, affected system or pathway, stop boundary, and continuation risk. | The review cannot show what was stopped or why continuing was unsafe. |
| Effective refusal restoration | Named refusal holder, authority scope, practical intervention pathway, and result-changing capability. | Refusal may be apparent rather than effective. |
| Corrective controls | Controls that address the specific mechanism that weakened refusal, evidence, correction, rollback, or review. | The process may return to the same path under a new label. |
| Controlled re-entry | Scope limit, time limit, monitoring owner, rollback or renewed stop condition, unresolved gaps. | Re-entry cannot be distinguished from full restart. |
| Anti-hollowing resistance | Evidence absence handling, conflict review, ritualization check, and appearance-prohibition check. | Procedural hollowing may be treated as validity. |
| Long-term continuity | Succession plan, retention schedule, periodic review trigger, and updates after organizational or technical change. | The process may work once and then decay into ceremony. |

## Practical Use Sequence

The [Practical Use Sequence](./EN_L30_RETURN_TO_REVERSIBILITY_PRACTICAL_USE_SEQUENCE.md) shows when this matrix should be used relative to the guidance and implementation checklist. Use this matrix whenever the checklist cannot identify a responsible owner or evidence source.

## Minimum use rule

Use this matrix before treating any return path as procedurally defensible. If roles are unnamed, evidence is missing, or re-entry can be lifted by the same interests that benefit from rapid return, the process should remain unresolved.

**Do not use this matrix as certification.** It is a non-binding public reference for making responsibility and evidence visible before controlled re-entry is considered.
