Return-to-Reversibility Implementation Checklist

A non-binding checklist for turning incident review into controlled, evidence-based return to reversibility.

This checklist does not authorize re-entry, certify safety, or determine legal compliance. It helps reviewers ask whether a stopped or paused trajectory has restored effective human refusal, evidence, corrective controls, and the ability to stop again before any controlled re-entry is treated as procedurally defensible.

How to use this checklist

Use this page after a boundary concern, incident review, pause, or stop condition has been identified. Each checked item should be backed by evidence, not merely assertion.

Recommended review record: for each section, record Evidence, Gap, Action, Owner, and Re-entry status.

Practical Use Sequence

Use the Practical Use Sequence when the review needs a simple order: identify the boundary concern, preserve evidence, use this checklist, assign roles with the matrix, decide re-entry state, and keep long-term monitoring active.

Role and Evidence Matrix

Use the Return-to-Reversibility Role and Evidence Matrix when a review must identify who can maintain the stop, who preserves evidence, who restores effective refusal, who reviews re-entry, and what evidence makes those roles real rather than nominal.

1. Stop trigger and scope

2. Evidence preservation

3. Restore effective refusal

4. Boundary diagnosis and corrective controls

5. Controlled re-entry only

6. Adversarial hollowing resistance

7. Long-term continuity

Review outcome

OutcomeUse this when
Do not re-enterEffective refusal, evidence, corrective controls, or renewed stop conditions remain missing or unverifiable.
Limited controlled re-entry onlyMinimum conditions are reviewable, but monitoring, scope limits, and rollback conditions remain necessary.
Continue monitoringRe-entry has begun, but long-term reversibility, evidence retention, and anti-hollowing checks must remain active.
Boundary rule: if actual refusal cannot be distinguished from apparent refusal, the process has not returned to reversibility.
